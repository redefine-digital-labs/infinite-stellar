# Infinite Stellar World Bible

## Canon statement

Infinite Stellar is not one endless universe. It is an endless passage through finite universes.

Each universe opens from a sealed stellar seed, develops through a declared season, and collapses at a known boundary. Civilizations, territory, energy, fleets, and score belong to that universe and do not survive it. Souls are the only player-facing identities that can cross the collapse.

The foundational line is:

> Universes reset. Civilizations disappear. The Soul remembers.

The game is about the tension between two needs:

> Survival rewards silence. Meaning requires being seen.

## The stellar condition

Every seasonal universe is covered by **the Veil**, an information law that prevents direct observation of complete space. A civilization can search nearby coordinates, recognize mathematical traces, and prove a valid route, but it cannot ask an omniscient map service where every planet is.

The Veil creates four kinds of scarcity:

- **Position:** a coordinate is dangerous when another civilization resolves it.
- **Attention:** exploration consumes player time and bounded local work.
- **Trust:** another player may share a route, a decoy, or only part of the truth.
- **Timing:** an arrival that is correct now may be disastrous when it lands.

The Veil is not supernatural encryption inside the fiction. It is the observable structure of reality. Civilizations build cultures around how much of themselves they reveal.

## Souls

A Soul is a persistent digital life that enters many seasonal universes. A connected address authorizes entry by proving current canonical ownership, but the address is the command key rather than the traveler.

It is simultaneously:

1. **A character** with recognizable expression, temperament, and voice.
2. **A relationship node** that can become known as ally, rival, witness, or mentor.
3. **A career archive** around which independently verifiable season records can be presented.

A Soul is not:

- the wallet that signs;
- the current avatar image;
- the player's civilization;
- a permanent combat unit;
- an accumulated bundle of ranked modifiers;
- an authority to control a Season Seat after ownership changes.

The holder chooses which Soul enters a season. The protocol determines whether that address is currently authorized and whether its one ranked controller slot remains unused. The Soul supplies continuity; the fixed Season Seat supplies control, Planet custody, and accountability.

## Embodiment

Souls do not enter a universe as abstract identifiers. They take visible form through accepted visual material and a temporary seasonal role through a **Commander Projection**.

Animacraft is the intended source of versioned visual composition and provenance for the command portrait, map presence, signal language, fleet skin, and post-season presentation. Infinite Stellar validates the display authority, freezes the accepted visual reference and fallback, and records them inside the Commander Projection's Soul/Seat/role binding at enrollment. The Animacraft artifact is not itself the game role or an authorization object.

The projection expresses identity but never changes ranked math. A rare part, expensive Soul, elaborate recipe, protected asset, or historical costume cannot increase energy, range, defense, proof speed, sponsorship priority, or score.

If the selected presentation later becomes unavailable or its display rights no longer apply, historical facts remain visible through a permanent neutral fallback.

## The seasonal arc

### 1. Embodiment

The holder authorizes entry with the current-owner address, chooses a Soul and visual, reviews public exposure, and atomically binds them to a new fixed-controller Season Seat. The resulting Civilization begins `AwaitingHome`, with no Planet. Every ranked Seat begins with the same strategic budget, and one address may create at most one ranked Seat per league and season.

### 2. Silent Dawn

The universe opens and its seed makes local search possible. Players may prepare privately while the onchain claim gate is not yet open or is paused. Once claiming is canonically available, a finalized `claim_home` creates the Seat-owned Founding Planet and changes the Civilization from `AwaitingHome` to `Active`. Most civilizations do not yet know whether they are alone.

### 3. First Contact

Public commitments and arrivals reveal that other civilizations exist. Players make first attacks, rescues, trades of information, and promises. Exact coordinates remain private unless discovered, shared, or leaked.

### 4. Signal War

The universe becomes a network of partially understood intentions. Players expand, reinforce, misdirect, coordinate, and contest routes. Public actions create evidence, but evidence does not explain motive.

### 5. The Last Light

A fresh, one-way onchain random transition activates a public Beacon objective. Controlling it creates a strategic advantage and an information liability: the player seeking recognition must become easier to find.

The endgame is deliberately legible. A season concludes with an onchain winner or a declared no-winner result, never an operator-selected interpretation.

### 6. Collapse

The declared season boundary freezes or settles remaining state. Civilizations, energy, fleets, technology, map advantage, and ranked power end with the universe.

### 7. Chronicle

Infinite Stellar emits factual Seat and Soul-segment records. A presentation layer may derive a story from those facts, but the current holder decides whether to submit any narrative into a supported Soulidity memory path.

The Echo is evidence. The Chronicle is interpretation.

## Narrative truth

Infinite Stellar separates three kinds of statements:

### Canonical facts

These are determined by the season package and checkpoint history:

- a Seat controlled a planet at a given transition;
- an arrival reinforced or attacked;
- a Soul binding remained valid for a bounded action segment;
- a Beacon result finalized;
- a season settled under a specific manifest.

### Derived descriptions

These are reproducible interpretations of canonical facts:

- “rescued three allied planets”;
- “held the Beacon for the longest eligible interval”;
- “encountered the same rival in three universes.”

The derivation rule and evidence links must remain visible.

### Subjective narrative

These include motive, personality, morality, and emotional interpretation. They may be authored by a player or assisted by an AI system, but must never masquerade as protocol fact. A Soul is not automatically labeled loyal, cruel, cowardly, or deceptive from ordinary telemetry.

## Tone

The tone is **cosmic mystery, strategic intimacy, and quiet consequence**.

Infinite Stellar is not limited to horror or a death-game aesthetic. The universe can be beautiful, social, melancholic, or hopeful while preserving uncertainty. Its emotional palette includes:

- awe before an unexplored field;
- tension when a commitment appears nearby;
- relief when reinforcement arrives;
- doubt about another Soul's promise;
- grief when a temporary civilization closes;
- anticipation when the same Souls meet again in a different universe.

## Visual and audio direction

The visual language should emphasize:

- deep negative space rather than a crowded dashboard;
- luminous points, paths, and signal interference;
- distinct Animacraft projections without turning the map into a portrait gallery;
- visible separation between public chain facts and private local knowledge;
- seasonal palettes that change without changing core information semantics;
- the Last Light as an unmistakable public focal point.

Sound should behave like information: sparse at first, increasingly layered as contact grows, and briefly silent at collapse. A Soul may have an identifiable motif, but gameplay-critical alerts use stable, accessible cues.

## Originality boundary

Infinite Stellar may learn from the hidden-information and fully onchain design space demonstrated by Dark Forest, but it does not adopt Dark Forest's name, fiction, art, prose, source code, circuits, or branded terminology by default.

The project owns its central fiction:

- Souls cross an infinite succession of finite stellar worlds.
- Accepted Animacraft visuals give those Souls visible form, while Commander Projections bind their temporary roles.
- civilizations reset to protect competitive equality;
- factual Echoes survive as verifiable history;
- the Last Light turns visibility into the final strategic choice.

## Canon change policy

This file defines stable product canon, not every seasonal story. A season may introduce new regions, relics, objectives, and presentation themes if it preserves the core identity model and labels temporary lore as seasonal.

Changes to Soul persistence, transfer meaning, ranked power, narrative truth, or the finite-season premise require an explicit decision-log update.
